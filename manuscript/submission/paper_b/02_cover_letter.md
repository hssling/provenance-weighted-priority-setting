# Cover letter

To the Editors, *International Journal of Epidemiology*

Dear Editors,

I am submitting for your consideration "Restated, never revised: a vintage analysis of WHO tuberculosis data and the spending estimate built on them".

**What the paper reports.** WHO's country-reported tuberculosis data are used for comparison, modelling and, by WHO itself, to estimate TB funding in low- and middle-income countries. Each value is read as that year's report. Using WHO's own archived releases and the code behind its published estimates, the paper measures how far that holds. None of 16,521 utilisation values changed between releases: the published history is never revised. Within releases, 62.2-71.3% of hospital and clinic utilisation values repeated the country's previous-year value, against 4.8-10.9% for expenditure lines. Where countries reported nothing, WHO's pipeline filled the gap from up to four years earlier or later. These utilisation values are the inputs to WHO's estimate of inpatient and outpatient care spending, which WHO counts as domestic funding. For 2025 that estimate was US$ 1.08 billion of US$ 6.57 billion, and only 32.6% of it rests on inputs newly reported for 2025. India's US$ 182 million rests on values last reported for 2022 and unchanged since 2020.

**Why it belongs in this journal.** The paper brings the real-time-data idea of vintage, standard in economics, to a widely used public health dataset, and measures it directly rather than inferring it. The reconstruction reproduces WHO's published totals exactly, so the result concerns the figure WHO publishes, not an approximation of it. The remedy is cheap: WHO's pipeline already knows which values it filled, and a per-value flag recording whether each was re-measured, restated or imputed would make the vintage of every number visible to analysts.

**What it deliberately does not claim.** Restatement is not error: a stable practice yields identical values honestly, and the analysis cannot tell a re-confirmed value from one carried forward unexamined, which is exactly what the proposed flag would record. Only three dated releases of the finance data are archived, so revision can be assessed over two intervals. A screen of all 443 numeric variables in six WHO datasets is included to show that the six cost inputs are the most estimate-like variables WHO's own dictionary labels; its conventions and failure modes are reported in full.

**Declarations.** The work is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has not been published in whole or in part. There is a single author. There is no funding and no competing interest. Ethical approval was not required: the study uses published aggregate data only.

**Related work by the same author, disclosed in full.** A companion paper analysing India's tuberculosis financing uses one of the same WHO datasets and is being submitted separately, to the Indian Journal of Tuberculosis. The two are written to stand alone, and a text-overlap screen between them returns 0.5%. A separate manuscript on drug-resistant tuberculosis outcomes in India (IJTB-S-26-01305) is under review at the same journal. I will inform you of any change in the status of either.

**Data and code.** All public inputs, the analysis code and a verification suite are deposited at https://github.com/hssling/provenance-weighted-priority-setting and archived at Zenodo, doi:10.5281/zenodo.24214490. WHO's report-repository files, which carry no licence, are fetched from pinned commits and verified by digest rather than redistributed. Every number in the paper is regenerated by the code.
