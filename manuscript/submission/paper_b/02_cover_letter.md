# Cover letter

To the Editors, *International Journal of Epidemiology*

Dear Editors,

I am submitting for your consideration "The label says what kind, not how old: a reproducible screen of 443 variables in six WHO tuberculosis datasets".

**What the paper reports.** Secondary analysts join WHO's country-reported tuberculosis files on column names. The variable definitions live in a separate dictionary, and that dictionary tells a reader what kind of quantity a column holds — six variables are labelled "Estimated" or "Typical" — but not whether any particular value was re-measured for that year or carried forward from the last one. The second property is what determines whether a trend analysis is possible, and no field in any file records it. For `hospd_prct_mdr`, the share of drug-resistant patients hospitalised, 69.6% of consecutive country-year pairs are identical and 42% of the 178 countries with four or more years never change their entry.

Screening all 443 numeric variables from the published files alone separates two kinds of column. Quantities a programme counts from its own records repeat rarely; quantities that characterise its standing configuration repeat often — a median of 63.0% in the laboratory services file against 13.0% to 15.6% in the budget, expenditure and notification files. The division survives the obvious objection. Most flagged variables are zero-dominated, and a repeated zero is an absence rather than a restated estimate; but excluding pairs in which both values are zero the gap widens rather than closes, and the file with the highest repetition turns out to have the fewest zero-dominated variables of any in the set.

**Why it belongs in this journal.** The paper is about what secondary analysts can and cannot infer from a widely used public dataset. The screen is generic — it needs only a country-year table and runs in seconds — and the remedy is a per-value vintage flag in the data file, which would cost the publisher little.

**What it deliberately does not claim.** The screen detects signatures consistent with non-measurement; it cannot prove that any particular value was carried forward rather than re-measured and found unchanged. The counted/characterised division was found post hoc, in one programme, at one point in time. The paper states all of this. It also records two claims from its own earlier drafts that the analysis then overturned, including one in which the screen was measuring repeated zeros rather than repeated estimates. A methods paper about data traps that conceals the ones its author fell into is worth less, so those corrections are reported in the text rather than removed from it.

**Declarations.** The work is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has not been published in whole or in part. There is a single author. There is no funding and no competing interest. Ethical approval was not required: the study uses published aggregate data only.

**Related work by the same author, disclosed in full.** A companion paper analysing India's tuberculosis financing shares one of these six datasets and is being submitted separately to a different journal. The two are written to stand alone, and a text-overlap screen between them returns 0.4%. A separate manuscript on drug-resistant tuberculosis outcomes in India (IJTB-S-26-01305) is in press at the Indian Journal of Tuberculosis, assigned to a forthcoming issue. I will inform you of any change in the status of either.

**Data and code.** All inputs are public WHO files. The archived copies as downloaded, the screening script, the per-variable results table and a verification suite are deposited at https://github.com/hssling/provenance-weighted-priority-setting and archived at Zenodo, doi:10.5281/zenodo.24187367. Every number in the paper is regenerated by that code.

**Use of generative AI.** I used a large language model as a drafting and analysis assistant. Every analytical decision, every verification and every conclusion is mine, and I take full responsibility for the content.
