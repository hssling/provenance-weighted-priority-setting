# Cover letter

To the Editors, *Tropical Medicine & International Health*

Dear Editors,

I am submitting for your consideration, as an original research article, "Restated, never revised: a vintage analysis of WHO tuberculosis data and the spending estimate built on them".

**What the paper reports.** The World Health Organization's country-reported tuberculosis data are the standard source for comparing TB programmes in low- and middle-income countries. WHO also uses them to estimate how much those countries spend on TB. Each value is read as that year's report. Using WHO's own archived releases and the code behind its published estimates, the paper tests that assumption.
- None of 16,521 utilisation values changed between releases.
- Within releases, 62.2-71.3% of hospital and clinic utilisation values repeated the country's previous-year value, against 4.8-10.9% of expenditure lines.
- Where countries reported nothing, WHO filled the gap from up to four years earlier or later.

These utilisation values feed WHO's estimate of inpatient and outpatient care spending, which WHO counts as domestic funding. For 2025 that estimate was US$ 1.08 billion, and the following hold:
- Only 32.6% of it rests on inputs newly reported for 2025.
- 52.4% rests on inputs unchanged for five years or more.
- Where countries did re-measure these inputs, they usually fell. Moving the old inputs at the observed rates lowers the estimate by 21.7%.
- In 77 of 115 countries, including 22 of the 31 high TB burden countries with an estimate, less than half of the estimate rests on inputs reported for the year.

**Why it belongs in this journal.** The paper concerns the data that national programmes, donors and WHO use to judge domestic financing of TB care in low- and middle-income countries. The countries most affected are those the journal's readers work in, India among them. The reconstruction reproduces WHO's published totals, so the findings concern the figure WHO publishes, not an approximation of it. The remedy is cheap. WHO's pipeline already knows which values it filled, so it could add a per-value flag recording whether each was re-measured, restated or imputed.

**What it deliberately does not claim.** Restatement is not error: a stable practice yields identical values honestly. The analysis cannot tell a re-confirmed value from one carried forward unexamined, which is exactly what the proposed flag would record. The re-measurement adjustment is a sensitivity bound, not a corrected estimate. The analyses of input age and the adjustment were specified in a dated plan before they were computed, and the plan is archived with the code. Only three dated releases of the finance data are archived, so revision can be assessed over two intervals only.

**Authorship and local involvement.** The study collected no data in any country. It analyses only published, aggregated, country-level data from WHO's global database, covering every country WHO includes. The author is a public health physician and researcher based in India, the country with the largest single share of the estimate.
