## D. Claims that need a citation and for which I found none

Paper A
1. §1 para 1: "It appears in investment cases, in advocacy, and in the comparative tables that shape where external financing goes." The Lancet Commission (ref 3) supports investment cases only in general. I found no verifiable source showing that the WHO funding gap in particular drives Global Fund allocation. Verify against Global Fund allocation-methodology documents, which are not DOI items and which I did not fetch.
2. §3.1/§4.1: that India's `cf_tot` equals `budget_tot` by convention. This is your own finding. WHO's reporting instructions or the data dictionary entry for `cf_tot` should be cited. I did not fetch it.
3. §3.4: the 37% to 1% Global Fund share from 2017 to 2025 is your own analysis of WHO data. Refs 5 to 7 give context only; none reaches 2025.
4. §4.2 para 2: "an indicator built to describe the external financing landscape has diminishing purchase on a domestically financed programme." No source found. I found no study on the validity of WHO's TB funding-gap indicator, and I found no cross-country comparability literature specific to health-financing indicators.
5. §4.2 para 3: that services and systems interventions are "persistently under-resourced relative to commodities" because of budget structure. No source found; you present it as a plausible mechanism, which is the right framing without one.
6. §3.5: sources for the intervention definitions (airborne infection control, ACF, bovine TB and so on). The evidence ranking is in the IJTB manuscript (C2).
7. §3.3: absorptive capacity as an explanation for under-execution. I found no health-specific absorptive-capacity paper I could verify. Ref 12 covers under-execution generally.
8. Two health-financing data sources discussed in the brief, the OECD/WHO/Eurostat System of Health Accounts and the Global Fund transition policy, are non-DOI documents. I did not fetch either, so I include neither.

Paper B
1. §3.1/§4.1: the claim that "no prior work screens these datasets" (§1 para 3) is an absence claim. I searched Crossref for TB-specific routine data-quality work and found only a Botswana preprint, which I did not include. State it as "to our knowledge".
2. §2.2 S1/S2: repetition and round-number clustering as signatures of estimation. I found no verifiable source on carried-forward values or heaping applied to country-reported programme data. Ref 17 is analogical. Digit-preference papers exist (for example Crockett 2001 and Pandit 2026 in Crossref) but concern census age heaping, and I did not include them.
3. §4.2: the benign reading that reporters sensibly restate standing configuration. This is inference, and you say so in §5. No source found.
4. §3.4: the point that expenditure and receipts differ in timing. Needs the WHO data dictionary definitions of `exp_tot` and `rcvd_tot`, which I did not fetch.
5. §3.3: Indian or global DR-TB hospitalisation norms. Refs 18 to 20 cover effectiveness of hospital versus ambulatory models, not current WHO policy; the 2026 WHO Module 4 guideline (already verified in the project) may be the right cite for current policy.
