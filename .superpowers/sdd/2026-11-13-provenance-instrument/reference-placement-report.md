## The Molnar / repetition-heuristic call (Paper B s2.2)
I presented S1 and S2 as **our own operationalisation justified by face validity** and did not cite Molnar. Reasons: (1) LOCF in dementia trials concerns analyst-imputed values, a different data-generating process from a country restating a standing answer, so it supports at most the near-definitional point that a repeated value is not a new measurement; (2) a reviewer who sees a trial-methodology citation will reasonably ask whether the heuristic was validated, and the honest answer is no; (3) the paper already says in Limitations that the screen lacked prior validation. The text now says so directly in s2.2, states "we take" and "we assume" for the repetition and round-number premises, and says the premise has not been validated against values of known provenance. Digit-preference literature was not cited (census age heaping is a different process).

## Under-review citation (Table 2 ranking): flag
Many journals disallow under-review citations for load-bearing evidence, and BMJ Global Health's policy should be checked. Is Table 2's ranking load-bearing? Partly. The count (six of eleven absent, two full, three partial) and the procurement-versus-delivery argument do not depend on the ranking. The headline "three of the top five" (abstract, s3.5, s6) does. More important, I read the IJTB manuscript: its Table 2 is a priority-action score (favourability lower bound x worsening slope x coupling weight) built from vocabulary-coded abstracts, 44% with no abstract, no human validation, and it reports that rank order changes under a coding rule. Paper A's "evidence-ranked"/"independently derived evidence score" wording overstated that. I fixed s2.3 and s5, but the abstract and the Table 2 caption still say "evidence-ranked". Recommend either relabelling to "ranked by a literature-favourability score" or giving the ranking its own methods paragraph, and choosing which of those before submission.
